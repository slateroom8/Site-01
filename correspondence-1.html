Correspondence with Air Canada

Subject: Best New Restaurants — question about panel independence

Kyle Williams to Air CanadaOctober 1, 2026

Hello,

Please forward this email to the Air Canada team responsible for overseeing the Best New Restaurants program.

I am writing to raise a concern about a business relationship between DINR, a collaborator in the program, and Lindsey Brennan, who is listed on the nomination panel.

Ms Brennan is the founder of Importations Vin i Vida. Published terms for a Spring Wine Bundles offer marketed by Somm and DINR identify ‘Tinc Set x Vin i Vida’ ad the Québec fulfilment partner. DINR has also published editorial content contributed by Ms. Brennan.

The program’s published eligibility criteria state that restaurants must be nominated by a panelist ‘without conflicts of interest’. In light of these documented connections, could you clarify:

  • was this commercial relationship disclosed to Air Canada before Ms. Brennan’s appointment?
  • who selected and approved her participation on the panel?
  • what safeguards address relationships between panelists and DINR, as well as any commercial ties panelists have to restaurants they nominate?

I am not alleging that this relationship influenced any result. However, it raises a reasonable question about how independence and potential conflicts are assessed and disclosed.

Some other nominated restaurants and businesses owned or represented by panelists have commercial relationships with DINR; could you clarify how these relationships were disclosed and assessed to preserve the independence of the selection process?

I would appreciate a written response from the team responsible for the program.

Thank you.
Kyle Williams

Air Canada representative to Kyle WilliamsOctober 7, 2026

Hi Kyle,

Thank you for your patience while we reviewed your questions. DINR is a partner to Air Canada and supports the Air Canada’s Best New Restaurants program with editorial guidance, program logistics and industry expertise. Air Canada retains oversight of the program.

Panelists are selected based on a range of considerations, including their knowledge of Canada’s culinary landscape, credibility within the industry and geographic representation. As part of the development of the 2026 panel, Air Canada and DINR reviewed potential conflicts of interest alongside these broader considerations. Lindsay Brennan was recommended through this process and her participation on the panel was approved by Air Canada.

Panel nominations are one input into a broader selection process. Nominated restaurants are subsequently reviewed and vetted before the finalist list is finalized with Air Canada’s oversight. Potential conflicts involving panelists and nominated restaurants are considered as part of that process.

We appreciate you reaching out with your questions.

Correspondence with DINR

Subject: Request for response before publication: DINR and Air Canada’s Best New Restaurants

Kyle Williams to DINROctober 22, 2026

Hello,

I am preparing a public article about potential conflicts of interest involving DINR’s role in Air Canada’s Best New Restaurants program. Before publication, I am giving DINR an opportunity to address the following concerns directly.

DINR participates in the program while maintaining commercial relationships with nominated restaurants and businesses associated with panelists. Public examples include reservation listings for nominees Alfred’s, Sammarco, Rita Trattoria and Nui, and a ticketed Kavita event offered through DINR. These relationships raise questions about the separation between DINR’s commercial interests and its influence over restaurant selection.

Please answer the following:

  • What authority does DINR have over panel appointments, nominations, finalist selection and the final Top 10 ranking?
  • Which relationships with nominees existed before nomination or evaluation, and how were those relationships disclosed and assessed?
  • What safeguards prevent DINR’s existing clients or prospective restaurant partners from receiving preferential consideration?
  • What relationships involving Lindsay Brennan, her restaurants and Vin i Vida were disclosed and assessed? Were any restrictions or recusals required?
  • Can the same person nominate a restaurant and subsequently evaluate it for the final ranking? If so, what independent review applies?
  • Who reviews potential conflicts involving DINR itself, independently of DINR’s commercial team?

I am requesting an explanation of governance and safeguards, rather than confidential scores, unpublished rankings or the identity of an anonymous reviewer.

Air Canada has stated that its investigation identified no conflict of interest, but declined to explain how the specific relationships were assessed. That conclusion will be included in the article. DINR’s own explanation is also relevant and will be represented fairly.

Please provide a substantive response by October 29, 2026. If additional time is needed, please explain why and propose a response date before that deadline. If no response is received, the article will state that DINR was approached for comment and did not respond by the publication deadline.

Regards, Kyle
